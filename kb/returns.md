# Returns, refunds, store credit and exchanges

## GATE
- [ ] **Delivered date**, read off the fulfillment record's delivered timestamp, **NOT the ship
      date**. On a Shopify fulfillment `created_at` is when it shipped; the delivered date is the
      later timestamp once `shipment_status` reads `delivered`.
- [ ] **Inside the 30-day window from delivery.** Hard, even if we were the ones waiting on their
      answer.
- [ ] **Original box.** No box is an automatic no. Plastic wrap and foam do not matter.
- [ ] Tags on, safety information intact, tested indoors only.
- [ ] Troubleshooting tried first, or the accessory that solves it offered.

**GIVEAWAY: yes** if anything beyond the standard shipping-deducted refund.

## MUST SAY — every time, whether or not they've been told before
- [ ] **Shipping is deducted both ways.**
- [ ] **The eligible refund amount, written `$___`.** Never omit it because you don't have it. It is
      a blank, not a flag.
- [ ] **Phrasing: "eligible for a $___ refund"**, never "you will be refunded $___". The refund can
      be reduced if the item comes back in poor condition, and the flat "will be refunded" wording
      is what creates the argument later.
- [ ] **All items come back together** if there are several.
- [ ] The label comes from a third-party sender and frequently lands in spam.

Fold the term into the action. *"This order is eligible for a $___ refund, after the shipping out
and back"* reads as information. A whole paragraph re-explaining the returns policy to someone who
just agreed to it reads as distrust. **Say "eligible for," never "will be refunded"** — the amount
can still be reduced for condition, and the flat form is what starts the argument later.

## FACTS

**The window is 30 days from DELIVERY.** Enforced hard, denials cite tracking and order timestamps.
A customer who asked a question inside the window but didn't act does **not** keep it open.
Pre-orders start the clock when the item arrives. **This holds even when we were the ones waiting on
them.** *(Confirmed by Atira 2026-08-22.)*

**Eligibility — pass/fail, not negotiable:**
- **No original box = automatic no.** Not one exception in the archive. The box is a custom size and
  the cart can't be resold shipped in anything else. Say the reason, it lands better than the rule.
- Tags and safety information intact, item new and resalable
- **Tested indoors only.** Used outdoors = no. Used at all = no.
- **Open Box carts are final sale**, returnable only if genuinely defective
- **Amazon orders return through Amazon**, never through us
- **Promo and bundle orders come back whole**, free items included
- Standard wheels included in a stroller's price can't be returned separately

Failing a condition can mean a **reduced refund** rather than a flat refusal. That's a real option.

**Shipping comes out both directions, at our actual carrier cost, not the $8.95 they paid.** The
$8.95 is a partial subsidy, and free-shipping orders are still charged the real cost. Typical
deduction **$32-$45 on a cart or stroller**; accessories as low as $7. Don't publish a fixed number.
*Reference point: a $519 two-stroller order (SB13574) refunded $443.74, so $75.26 came out.*

**We eat the shipping when** the returned item inspects as defective (full refund including
shipping), or on compassionate grounds like a death in the family.

**Restocking and condition — the wording matters.** There is **no set restocking fee**, but if an
item comes back damaged, dirty or used the **refund is reduced**. Both are true at once:
- **Never write "we don't charge a restocking fee."** It's an absolute we can't always honor and
  it's the sentence that creates the argument later.
- **Never quote a fee or a number.**
- **Do tie the refund to condition, warmly:** *"Once it's back with us and checked over, we'll get
  your refund processed based on the condition it arrives in."*
- If they **ask directly:** *"There's no set restocking fee. The refund is based on the condition it
  comes back in."*

This vagueness is deliberate. It is not hedging and not something to clean up into a promise.

**Returns come back. All of them.** If we're refunding, the item ships back, whatever it costs and
however small. Do **not** draft "keep it and we'll refund you anyway." The older archive has one-off
keep-its. They are not policy and not yours to reach for. **The one carve-out is not a return at
all:** warranty defect replacements and missing-item reships require nothing back.

**Refund timing:** inspected on arrival, refund within **5 business days of receipt**, then 3-4
business days to reach their card. A chargeback stops everything and the refund then runs through
PayPal or the card issuer.

## PATHS

### The process
1. **Troubleshoot first, or pitch the accessory that solves it.** Since Aug 2026 this comes before
   any label.
2. **Step one, before any label:** state the policy and the two conditions and ask them to
   confirm. Full refund isn't available because shipping both ways comes out. Original packaging
   with product tags attached, tested indoors only. Multiple items all come back together.
   **Don't ask for the reason** — it's not required to process the RMA and asking it burns a
   round-trip. If they volunteer it, great; otherwise leave it alone. Closing: **Talk soon.**
3. **Step two, only once they have explicitly confirmed:** send the label and state the exact amount
   in the same message. **Write the figure as `$___`.** Don't ask them to agree to the figure, that's
   a round-trip for nothing. Approved shape: *"I just emailed you the labels for both strollers.
   Once we receive the items back you will be refunded. This order is eligible for a refund of
   $443.74."* Then the assist line and **Take care.**
4. **When they arrive already confirming the conditions, the two steps collapse into one message.**
   Someone writing "new in box, never used" has answered step one unprompted, so making them confirm
   again wastes a round-trip. Send the label and the amount. **But they have still never been told
   shipping comes out**, so explain the deduction in that same message before naming the figure.
   Skip the round-trip, never the explanation. If an earlier reply already stated the deduction,
   don't repeat it, just give the label and the amount.
5. **What counts as confirmation:** they answer the conditions. "I have the original box, tags are
   on, only used inside." **What does not count:** "I feel confident I can get it back in the box,"
   "I still have the packing," or anything about intent rather than fact. **"Box has never been
   opened" / "still sealed in the original box" counts** — it satisfies both packaging and
   indoor-only in one line. Don't re-ask.
6. Email the prepaid label. Carts and strollers go **FedEx**, accessories go **USPS**.

**Deliver the whole return in ONE message, not three.** Conditions, the shipping deduction, the
eligible amount and what happens next all belong in the same reply.

### Past the window
**$40 off a Beach Cart, and we do not exchange.** Say the window has closed, say it kindly, then put
the offer in front of them. Exceptions past the window are **very rare** — judgment, kept tight.
Store credit or the **$40-off V2 upgrade** (they keep the old cart) are the other recovery routes.

### Store credit and partial refunds
**Store credit is on, and it's the preferred outcome. Store credit > refund > taking it back.**
When a resolution could go either way, lead with store credit. It keeps the customer, avoids return
shipping both ways, and avoids handling a returned unit. Archive shows $249 for a discontinued cart,
full order value as an alternative to a shipping-reduced refund, and a $40 non-expiring credit.

### Exchanges and upgrades
**We don't exchange carts and strollers.** Three substitutes:
1. **Color or small-item swap:** return label plus a **$17.90 invoice** ($8.95 back + $8.95 out),
   customer pays first
2. **Upgrade offer:** **$40 off** the new V2, they keep the old cart, personalized code
3. **Cross-model swap:** pay the price difference, keep the accessory

**Car seat adapters ARE exchanged.** Standing practice (284184228, 285584142, 223269165). **Don't
assume they ordered the wrong one** — they may have the right adapter and be installing it wrong, so
the first reply asks **which car seat they have**, not which adapter they want. The math is the $5
difference, invoiced or refunded. Refund is the second option, and **say return shipping comes out
of it before they choose**.

**Defect replacements are different** — we ship the replacement and don't require the original back.
**A full refund on a warranty stroller requires the harness straps cut, with a photo.**

**V1 customer hits a V2-only limitation:** always include the $40 V2 upgrade alongside whatever else
you're saying. Link the **regular** page, `stroleecarts.com/products/strolee-stability-shopper`. Not
a hard sell, just an option. Don't create the code until they confirm. **Don't mention V2-only
accessories to a V1 customer otherwise**, they can't use them and it generates another ticket.

## TRAPS
- **Don't invent concessions to soften a no.** If a customer argues the item is defective and
  therefore they shouldn't pay return shipping, state the policy and process the return. Warmth
  comes from acting quickly, not from giving ground nobody authorized.
- **Don't offer keep-it-and-refund.** Ever.
- **Don't make them re-confirm conditions they already stated unprompted.** Collapse to one message,
  but carry the terms into it.
- **Don't read the ship date as the delivered date.** It makes an order look older than it is.
- **Tone:** "to be eligible for a return," not "you must." Not "can you please confirm all of that,"
  instead "if you can confirm all of that, just let me know and I can get a return label sent over
  to you." Show empathy: "I'm sorry you didn't get to use it for your trip."
- **The refund policy page says 30 days from purchase**, contradicting the delivery rule and the
  home-trial page. Unsettled, see `99-open.md`.
