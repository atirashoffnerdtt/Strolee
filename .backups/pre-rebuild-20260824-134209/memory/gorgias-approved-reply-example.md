Approved and sent 2026-08-15, ticket 287899154 — customer couldn't get the Stability Shopper cross bar to click:

> Hi Elaine!
>
> Thank you for those photos that helps a lot, and I'm sorry for the runaround! You have the Stability Shopper, and the good news is you're not missing any hardware. There are no screws in step 3, so nothing is missing from your box!
>
> The cross bar locks onto the cart with small spring-loaded buttons on the underside of the bars. Slide the two halves of the cross bar together, then line the bar up over the button, press the button down, and slide it on until it's fully seated. Let go and it should click into place!
>
> If you don't see a button where it should be, it may have slipped down inside the tube during shipping. Just drop a pen into the top of the tube and give it a gentle push, and the button will pop right back out.
>
> They're the same kind of spring button that holds a collapsable tent pole together.
>
> If a button still won't stay up after that, let me know and I'll take care of it right away.
>
> Talk soon,
> Atira S
> The Strolee Team

What this example establishes:
- Warm and direct. Lead with reassurance ("nothing is missing from your box"), then the fix, then the fallback offer.
- Exclamation points are welcome; they carry the warmth.
- Short paragraphs, one idea each. Plain numbered-free prose when the steps are simple enough to run in a sentence.
- Plain everyday comparisons over technical part names.
- Always end with a real offer to make it right.

**Why:** Atira asked me to note this response as the benchmark after three rounds of edits on my drafts.
