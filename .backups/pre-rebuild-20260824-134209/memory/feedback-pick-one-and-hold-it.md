Atira's boss's standing complaint about the previous support agent: **he wished she would just pick something and stick with it.** Inconsistency was the thing that got noticed from outside, not the individual answers.

**Why:** An inconsistent policy is worse for customers and worse for Strolee than a merely adequate one. Customers compare notes. Two people getting different answers to the same question is what turns a support interaction into an argument, and it's what made the ticket archive contradict itself 23 different ways.

**The limit of this principle, in Atira's words:** *"We don't need to sacrifice a good customer experience for the sake of consistency. But also we can't be willy nilly."* Consistency is the default to reach for, not a rule that overrides judgment. The thing to avoid is being **random**, not being generous.

**How to apply:** Use the settled answer in PLAYBOOK.md Part 4 as the default. Never improvise a dollar amount or invent a fee. When a ticket genuinely warrants more than the default, give it more and say plainly in the draft write-up that it's above the usual and why — Atira reviews every draft anyway, she is the only person doing CS.

Settled so far (2026-08-21): goodwill/exceptions **$30**, anything beyond goes to Atira; **no set restocking fee** but refunds reflect condition, worded deliberately vaguely; **cut harness strap photo mandatory, no exceptions ever**.
