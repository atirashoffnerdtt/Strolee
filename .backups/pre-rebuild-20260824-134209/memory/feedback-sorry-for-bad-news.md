Atira corrected a draft on 2026-08-21 (Tiffany, Linq bar restock) with the note **"Please be nicer. Sorry about that! etc"**. My version stated the no cleanly and stopped. Hers added the apology and an alternative:

> The Linq bars are no longer available, and we won't be restocking them, **I'm sorry about that!** Our double stroller is more popular. Our Linq strollers are still available on their own, and they can be pushed side by side.

**Why:** The "never apologize" rule covers apologizing for a **defect**, for **product quality**, and for **slow service**. It does NOT cover the news itself. Telling a customer something is discontinued, out of stock, incompatible, or not something Strolee does is disappointing, and saying sorry about it is plain kindness with no liability attached.

**How to apply:** Any reply carrying a no gets three parts, in order: the answer plainly, a soft "I'm sorry about that!", and somewhere to go next. Never a bare no. Erring cold is the more common failure in my drafts than erring apologetic. See [[strolee-never-affirm-defects]] for what genuinely stays off-limits, and [[gorgias-sounds-like-ai]] for the standing warning not to strip warmth in the name of tightening.
